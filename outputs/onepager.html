A regime model that can say what happened — and a measurement of how much that is worth

Statistical regime detection with a citation-grounded explanation layer, and three controls that test it. BSc Mathematics with Statistics, University of Bristol.

A two-state Gaussian HMM, refitted inside each of 27 walk-forward folds and scored only on held-out days, separates SPY volatility regimes by 2.18× (95% CI 1.76–2.69); the same procedure refitted on three indices outside the original universe gives 1.54×, 1.46×, 1.87×. A 20-day persistence filter reduces 84 raw state flips to 24, of which 20 recur across random seeds. For each, a language model reading only news published before that date produced a citation-grounded explanation. Stripped of dates and scrambled, 55% matched back to the correct fortnight against 5% chance (p < 0.0001), with 96.4% of 474 claims grounded in the source they cite and zero fabricated citations. On era-matched non-transition dates the identical pipeline produced confident explanations 61.5% of the time against 80% on real transitions — a gap this study had only a 17% chance of detecting. That is the honest limit of the method.

The problem

A regime model tells you the market changed. It cannot tell you what happened, because it never sees anything except returns. So its output is coloured bands a human interprets from memory — undocumented, unauditable, and different for every person who looks. For a platform running thirty PMs, that is thirty private readings of one signal.

The split that makes it testable

The HMM decides when the regime changed. The language model only describes what was in the news at that moment. No number in the statistical results comes from the model, and it never moves a regime boundary.

Three corrections to the prior work

  • The earlier notebook's 2.32× was in-sample over the full history, and its 26 folds belonged to a separate return classifier. One sentence, two unrelated experiments. Refit per fold it is 2.18×.
  • States are assigned by a forward filter, not Viterbi — which smooths over the whole test block, letting a label depend on up to 126 days of future data and contaminating the very transition dates the news control rests on.
  • The scaler is fitted inside each fold.

Control 1 — hindsight

The window closes on the transition date, enforced structurally: a window object refuses to construct if any item post-dates it. 60 windows, 10,888 items, 0 retrieval failures, zero post-boundary survivors.

Why revision pinning is not optional. Wikipedia's page for a given day keeps being edited for years. Across sampled transition dates a mean of 38.2% of the current page was written after the boundary, peaking at 71.2% — the 2018 January 26 page was 1,266 bytes on the day and is 4,401 today, last edited in 2025. Fetching the live page would feed the model text written with full knowledge of what followed, while the manifest recorded the window as closing on the date. Every page is pinned by revision id.

Control 2 — memorisation

Dates are stripped before the model sees the text; every claim must cite a supplied item; grounding is scored by lexical overlap, so the check itself has no world knowledge. 96.4% grounded, 0.0% fabricated citations, 474 claims. Splitting either side of the training cutoff was abandoned: the post-cutoff arm holds three transitions, and reporting n=3 as a test is worse than not running it.

Fisher exact p = 0.43. Identical prompt, identical pipeline; only the news differs.

Not significant means not resolved, not absent. Power at the observed 18.5pp gap is 0.17 — a one-in-six chance of detecting the effect actually observed. Reaching 80% power with 20 transitions would need a near-100% transition rate, and the binding constraint is the number of transitions, not controls: even 200 controls only gets there at ~93%. The gap runs in the predicted direction and reproduces exactly on a full replicate run (κ = 0.84 on the confidence label; rates 0.8→0.8 and 0.65→0.65), so it is stable rather than noisy — but this design could not have proven it.
What is established. The explanations are specific to their window and faithful to their sources — including on business-and-economy text alone (28.6% vs 7.1% chance, p = 0.016). What is not established is that they are diagnostic of a regime change: the model finds a plausible story for an ordinary fortnight nearly as often.
